\section{Data and methods}
\label{sec:methods}

Hypotheses, decision rules and tests were written before each run, and
Appendix~\ref{app:ledger} lists every one of them with its verdict. They were also committed to
the repository before the run for the Paderborn experiments, both power gates, both learned-mask
runs and the later Ottawa runs on robust selectors and on cleaning after decimation; the commit
history records the order. The confirmatory Paderborn run of the fault-order rule came first, and
the two runs that vary its band and its acquisition were designed after it, on the same records.
The first Ottawa and CWRU runs were committed only after they had run. The spike-removal and
median arms on Ottawa were added after an exploratory analysis of the same records had shown their
effect, the CWRU endpoint was moved to the highest dose after a smoke run on two records, the
evaluability rule of the learned masks was fixed after the training diagnostics of a first
attempt, and the Ottawa power gate was run after the learned-mask run that it assesses. Values
given here are those of the experiment configurations, and the repository listed under Data and
code availability holds them.

\subsection{Datasets}
\label{sec:data}

\paragraph{Ottawa}
The University of Ottawa benchmark \citep{huang2018ottawa} records bearing vibration under
time-varying speed at 200~kS/s, with an ICP accelerometer (model 623C01) and an incremental shaft
encoder on a USB data-acquisition board; we count 1024 encoder pulses per revolution, a value
inferred from the data because it is not documented. It contains
healthy, inner-race and outer-race conditions under four speed scenarios, with three trials each.
The bearing has nine rolling elements and a diameter ratio $d/D = 0.2067$, so the fault orders are
$\mathrm{BPFO} = 3.570$ and $\mathrm{BPFI} = 5.430$. Records are cut into 2.5~s segments.

\paragraph{CWRU}
The drive-end 48~kS/s records of the Case Western Reserve University benchmark, with the
categorization of \citet{smith2015cwru}, host the injected-interference experiment.

\paragraph{Paderborn}
The Paderborn benchmark \citep{lessmeier2016paderborn} records 6203 bearings at 64~kS/s in a drive
fed by a PWM inverter with 16~kHz switching. We use the three settings at 1500~rpm, whose 4~s
measurements resolve 0.010 order, and the 23 bearings with single-race damage: 12 outer-race (7
artificial, 5 from accelerated lifetime tests) and 11 inner-race (5 artificial, 6 real). The
geometry of the FAG bearing (8 balls, $d = 6.75$~mm, $D = 28.55$~mm, contact angle $0^\circ$) gives
$\mathrm{BPFO} = 3.054$ and $\mathrm{BPFI} = 4.946$ orders; the manufacturers used vary by up to
1--2\% in characteristic frequencies \citep{lessmeier2016paderborn}.

The 64~kS/s record length varies between files while the stored time axis always spans 4~s. We
checked the time base with the motor current: the drive is a synchronous machine with four pole
pairs, so its electrical frequency is exactly four times the shaft speed, which is recorded on a
separate 4~kHz channel. With a fixed 64~kS/s, the ratio between the two has a median of 0.99998
over 2559 readable files, and only one file deviates by more than 0.5\%; the alternative reading,
in which the stored time axis is correct, puts 185 files beyond 0.5\%. We therefore use 64~kS/s
throughout, cut every measurement to 255\,936 samples (4~s minus 1~ms) aligned with the speed
channel, and exclude shorter or unreadable measurements.

\paragraph{A benchmark set aside}
A fourth benchmark, MAFAULDA, was analysed and set aside before any comparison of methods. Its
fault orders, 2.998 and 5.002, lie on the third and fifth shaft harmonics, and 59\% of the
full-rate estimates fall within 0.03 of an integer order, so an order hit there measures the shaft
harmonic rather than the fault.

\subsection{Acquisition model}

Sub-Nyquist acquisition is modelled by pure decimation, $x_M[n] = x[nM]$, of the full-rate record,
which is an ideal sampler at $f_s/M$ with no anti-alias filter. No track-and-hold bandwidth,
aperture jitter or quantization is modelled; the cost metric is the number of converted samples
per second. Because the records were acquired with the anti-alias filter of the original
instrument, content above the original Nyquist frequency is absent, and the folding studied here
is that of content between the effective and the original Nyquist frequencies.

\subsection{Classical pipeline}
\label{sec:pipeline}

The baseline is the classical envelope pipeline. The band is chosen by a fast kurtogram
\citep{antoni2007kurtogram} with ideal FFT band masks, dyadic and one-third-dyadic levels up to
level 4, and a search range from 500~Hz to a ceiling. The ceiling is the effective Nyquist
frequency for Paderborn and 25~kHz for Ottawa. On Ottawa it is a band prior, chosen after an
observation: without it, the full-rate kurtogram selects a band between 50 and 100~kHz in all
twelve outer-race records, and the fault appears at no order, although the content above 25~kHz
carries only 2 to 7\% of the record energy. The squared envelope of the
band is resampled in angle at 64 samples per revolution with an angular anti-alias filter, and the
order estimate is the maximum of the order spectrum above order 2, with no prior on the fault
order. A run is discarded, and counted as a miss, when the decimated record has fewer than 4096
samples, when the effective Nyquist frequency falls below the search floor, or when fewer than
$g \times 64$ samples per revolution remain, with $g = 2$ for Ottawa and $g = 1$ for Paderborn.

\subsection{Ground truth}
\label{sec:truth}

The fault order is known from geometry only up to slip and manufacturing spread, so the truth is
calibrated from the full-rate data, never from the arm under test.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Local ruler}: the order of the full-rate line in the same segment, refined around
  the geometric order by harmonic-energy score and taken at the centre of the score plateau. The
  search spans $\pm12\%$ for Ottawa and $\pm1\%$ for Paderborn. For Paderborn the line must also
  stand out: its peak must reach at least five times the median of the order spectrum within
  $\pm7\%$ of the geometric order. A segment without a valid line has no local truth.
  \item \emph{Nested ruler}: the median of the valid calibrations of other records of the same
  race, outside the test set. In E24, which has no training, the bearing under test is left out.
\end{itemize}
An estimate is a hit when it falls within 0.06 order of the truth for Ottawa and 0.025 order for
Paderborn. The tighter tolerance is forced by geometry: the Paderborn BPFO and BPFI lie 0.054 order
from the third and fifth shaft harmonics, and a looser tolerance would score a line locked on the
harmonic as a hit. Accuracy is balanced: the mean over races of the per-unit hit rate.

\subsection{Spike removal}
\label{sec:met-spikes}

A sample is an isolated spike when its deviation from the mean of its two neighbours,
$\delta[n] = x[n] - \tfrac12(x[n-1] + x[n+1])$, exceeds ten times
$1.4826\,\mathrm{median}|\delta|$, and both neighbours deviate in the opposite direction by less
than 0.8 of it. Spikes are replaced by the mean of their neighbours. Applied at full rate, before
decimation, the procedure is not realizable on a decimating node; it is a diagnostic intervention
that attributes the loss to the spikes. A single-sample spike that survives decimation lands on a
kept sample and stays isolated in the decimated stream, so the same criterion can also be applied
after decimation, at the effective rate, without access to the full-rate record. This variant is
tested as a separate arm, and so is a three-point running median applied to the decimated stream.
Both assume an ideal sampler: a track-and-hold of finite bandwidth would not record a
microsecond-scale event as an isolated sample at full amplitude.

\subsection{Attribution by folding zone}
\label{sec:zones}

At $4\times$ on Ottawa the effective Nyquist frequency is 25~kHz, equal to the search ceiling. The
upper half of the search range at the first kurtogram level, $[12.75, 25]$~kHz, then receives the
folded content of three zones of the original frequency axis: $Z_1 = [25, 37.25]$,
$Z_2 = [62.75, 75]$ and $Z_3 = [75, 87.25]$~kHz. To attribute the loss to zones, an aim later
withdrawn (Section~\ref{sec:res-valley}), each zone, and their
union, is rejected at full rate before decimation by an ideal zero-phase FFT mask. An ideal
anti-alias filter, which zeroes everything above the effective Nyquist frequency, and a
three-point running median complete the interventions. None of these masks is realizable; their
purpose is causal attribution.

\subsection{Robust band selectors}

Two selectors replace spectral kurtosis on the same band partition. The SES infogram
\citep{antoni2016infogram} computes, for each band, the energy flow $\varepsilon[n] = |c[n]|^2$ of
the analytic band signal sampled at its critical rate, and the negentropy of the squared-envelope
spectrum without its zero-frequency term,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta I_E = \left\langle \frac{|E(\alpha)|^2}{\langle |E|^2\rangle}
  \ln\frac{|E(\alpha)|^2}{\langle |E|^2\rangle} \right\rangle_{\alpha \neq 0},
\end{equation}
where $E$ is the discrete Fourier transform of $\varepsilon$. Our implementation reproduces the
values that \citet{antoni2016infogram} gives for stationary Gaussian noise (1.1525 for the
time-domain and 0.4228 for the spectral negentropy). The spectral Gini index
\citep{hebdasobkowicz2020nongaussian} replaces the kurtosis by the Gini index of the band envelope.

\subsection{Fault-order rule and acquisition variants}
\label{sec:met-rule}

On Paderborn we also replace the blind order estimate by a rule that looks only at the fault
orders of the bearing geometry, as in band selectors that target known cyclic frequencies
\citep{smith2019demodband,mauricio2020iesfogram,schmidt2020ifbigram}.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Score.} For each candidate fault (BPFO or BPFI) and band, the score is the
  prominence of the envelope order spectrum, its peak divided by its median, in a window of
  $\pm2\%$ around the first and the second harmonic of the geometric order, averaged over the two.
  Orders within $\pm0.015$ of an integer are excluded from the windows, because on Paderborn the
  fault orders lie 0.054 from the third and the fifth shaft harmonic.
  \item \emph{Decision.} The fault with the larger score is reported if that score exceeds the 95th
  percentile of the larger score on the healthy bearings (K001 to K006) at the same factor;
  otherwise the segment is declared healthy. A healthy bearing is evaluated with the threshold of
  the other five.
  \item \emph{Band.} The rule is applied to the band chosen by the kurtogram, to the whole band
  from 500~Hz to the effective Nyquist frequency, or to the band of the kurtogram partition
  (levels 1 to 3) that maximizes the score, which we call the guided band.
\end{itemize}
Accuracy is balanced over the two races on the damaged bearings, and specificity is the fraction
of healthy segments declared healthy.

The acquisition is varied at the same rate $f_s/M$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item pure decimation, $x[::M]$;
  \item an ideal anti-alias filter at $f_s/(2M)$ before decimation;
  \item an ideal band-pass filter on one Nyquist zone, $[k, k+1] \cdot f_s/(2M)$, before
  decimation, which is classical band-pass sampling \citep{vaughan1991,lu2018undersampled}. The
  zone is chosen either on the other bearings or from the full-rate kurtogram of a commissioning
  record.
\end{itemize}
The confirmation run, the run with the rule fixed and the band varied, and the acquisition run
were each pre-registered before they ran; the last two were designed after the first. The rule
looks only at two candidate orders and takes its threshold from healthy bearings, while the blind
pipeline estimates any order from 2 to 32, so the two operate at different specificities, and both
are reported. A pilot of the same rule on Ottawa was exploratory and was revised twice after seeing
the data; it enters only as a failure case (Section~\ref{sec:res-fail}).

\subsection{Statistical protocol}
\label{sec:stats}

The independent unit is the bearing on Paderborn, the condition-by-scenario cluster on Ottawa (four
per race) and the fault cluster on CWRU (four). Three tests are used, each named where it is
reported.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Cluster bootstrap} (learned masks, spike removal on Paderborn, CWRU). Units are
  resampled with replacement within each race, together with the cross-validation folds when there
  are any, and the one-sided p-value is $(1 + \#\{\Delta^* \le 0\})/(1 + B)$ with $B = 4000$, so a
  zero difference counts against the hypothesis. With few clusters this bootstrap is
  anticonservative.
  \item \emph{Exact sign-flip test} (fault-order rule, search ceiling, robustness checks on
  Paderborn). The statistic is the difference in balanced accuracy, the mean over races of the
  per-bearing differences. Its null distribution flips the sign of each bearing's difference
  independently: exactly, over all assignments, when at most 18 bearings differ, and with $10^6$
  random assignments otherwise. The test is one-sided unless stated, and 95\% intervals are
  obtained by inverting it.
  \item \emph{Exact McNemar test} (paired hits, two-sided), on segments, on records or on bearings
  identified by majority of segments.
\end{itemize}
Holm's correction is applied within each family of tests. On Ottawa and CWRU no comparison can be
significant at the level of the independent unit: with four clusters, the smallest one-sided p-value
of a sign-flip test is $1/16$. The Ottawa effects are therefore reported by segment and by record,
which treats these as independent and overstates the evidence.

\paragraph{Power gate}
We asked whether a benchmark can detect an improvement at all: on Paderborn before training, on
Ottawa after the learned-mask run. A planted arm is derived from the classical baseline by turning
each of its misses into a hit with probability $q$, using the same draw for both rulers, with $q$
chosen for gains of +5 and +10 points or for a perfect arm. The pre-registered decision rule is then
applied with all other comparisons assumed significant, which is deliberately optimistic, so that a
negative answer is conclusive. A benchmark passes if the power at +5 points reaches 0.80 in some
admissible design. Because the classical baseline is deterministic, the gate measures power only
against it; it says nothing about comparisons between learned arms, whose variance across seeds it
does not model.

\subsection{Learned sampling masks}
\label{sec:masks}

For a budget of one sample in $b$, the feasible set allows $K = 4$ active ticks in each period of
$L = 4b$ ticks, with a minimum gap of two ticks between them. This is the class of periodic
nonuniform patterns that a timer-triggered converter can schedule. Masks are learned by a top-$K$
Gumbel relaxation with a straight-through estimator and an annealed temperature. The mask is
trained jointly with an unrolled decoder \citep{monga2021unrolling} that mirrors the classical
pipeline:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a learnable complex filter bank;
  \item the squared envelope;
  \item angular resampling;
  \item order-spectrum logits.
\end{itemize}
The decoder has no learned term per order bin, so it cannot place a peak where the data have none.
Early stopping and checkpoint selection start only after the annealing ends. A budget is evaluable
only if the masks left the Gumbel noise, that is, if the margin between the $K$-th and
$(K{+}1)$-th logits exceeds the noise standard deviation, in at least half of the splits.

The learned masks are compared with:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the best uniform rate at or below the budget, chosen on training data;
  \item the same decoder with that uniform mask;
  \item a fixed random feasible mask;
  \item multicoset designs, including that of \citet{venkataramani2001optimal};
  \item a mask learned without the constraint and projected onto it afterwards
  \citep{huijben2020dps,weiss2021pilot}.
\end{itemize}

On Paderborn, cross-validation is by bearing, in four folds stratified by race, with three seeds
per fold. Training labels come from the nested ruler, because the local ruler exists for only 7 of
the 23 bearings.
